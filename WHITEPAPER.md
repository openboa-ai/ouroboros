# Ouroboros: An Agent-Native Trading System for Continuous Self-Improvement

## Abstract

Ouroboros is an agent-native quantitative firm whose terminal objective is to generate
economically worthwhile shareholder returns through authorized live trading of actual capital,
growing both distributions to the shareholder and retained shareholder capital. The continued use
of that capital must remain justified relative to realistic alternatives under the mandate.

It begins from the premise that markets are adaptive and continually changing. No observed trading
advantage can be presumed permanent. A firm seeking continuing shareholder value must therefore
be capable not only of trading, but of repeatedly transforming the consequences of trading into
valid changes to itself.

Agents are the primary actors throughout this closed loop. They trade, conduct research, develop
and evaluate alternatives, preserve learning, and replace the system's existing forms when
justified by evidence. Strategies, models, tools, code, and organizational structures remain
provisional. Hill climbing defines the direction of the process, while the methods of search and
improvement remain subject to optimization.

Self-improvement, however, is not self-certifying. Models, backtests, simulations, and paper
trading provide evidence of differing strength, but none can supersede market reality. No candidate
may unilaterally define or control the standards by which its own improvement is accepted. The
integrity of evidence, evaluation, selection, and succession is therefore constitutive of the
system rather than auxiliary to it.

Ouroboros combines extensive agent autonomy with retained human sovereignty. Humans govern the
terminal objective, capital boundaries, revocation, and the expansion of authority; agents
exercise the greatest autonomy possible within those boundaries. Security and operational
stability arise from this governed delegation, not from the indiscriminate suppression of agency.

This paper establishes the philosophical, epistemic, and governing foundations from which the
product specification and architecture of Ouroboros will be derived. It defines no final strategy,
organization, model, or method. The system is intended to remain capable of changing every internal
form except the doctrine and sovereign boundaries that make its continued evolution legitimate.

## Origin

Ouroboros originates within the broader vision of OpenBoa.

Humanity is defined not only by what it can understand, but by what it can imagine and realize.

OpenBoa exists to expand the horizon of human possibility. It recognizes agents as a new means
through which the scope of human creation can expand and explores that horizon through the
**Business of Agents**.

Ouroboros is one implementation of this vision. It is not a definition of OpenBoa as a whole, nor
does it reduce the horizon of human possibility to trading. It explores a particular frontier:
whether agents can become the primary actors in an economically consequential system that trades,
learns, develops, reorganizes, and improves itself under retained human sovereignty.

Trading makes this frontier concrete. Its consequences cannot be sustained by language or
appearance alone. Capital moves, markets respond, errors accumulate, and claims of improvement
eventually encounter external reality. Ouroboros therefore provides a demanding realization of
agent-native creation: an autonomous organization must produce economic consequence while
remaining capable of learning from it and responsible for it.

The name **Ouroboros** expresses this structure. Trading creates the consequences from which the
system changes itself, and the changed system returns to trade again. The loop is recursive but not
self-contained. Market reality remains outside it, continually testing whether internal
improvement has become improvement in the world.

Through Ouroboros, the Business of Agents is expressed not as the automation of an existing human
workflow, but as the creation of a system whose operation, development, and organization are
natively agentic. Human imagination establishes the purpose and sovereign boundary. Agents extend
the capacity to realize it.

## 1. The Problem of Impermanent Advantage

Financial markets are not stationary optimization environments. Opportunities emerge within
changing configurations of participants, incentives, information, liquidity, technology,
regulation, and collective behavior. The conditions that produce an observed advantage may change
as the market adapts, as competitors respond, or as the advantage itself is exploited.

This does not imply that every fixed strategy must fail immediately, nor that every change
constitutes progress. It means that permanence cannot be treated as a property of an observed
advantage. A trading firm seeking continuing shareholder value must remain accountable to the
possibility that the conditions supporting its past success no longer hold.

The problem is therefore larger than discovering a profitable strategy. Ouroboros must repeatedly
determine what remains valid, what has ceased to work, what new possibilities have emerged, and
which changes are sufficiently supported to affect actual capital. It must be capable of revising
not only its strategies, but also the models, tools, experiments, evaluators, organizational forms,
and development processes through which strategies are produced.

Treating trading and improvement as separate systems weakens this capacity. Trading then produces
economic consequences without necessarily preserving them as learning, while research risks
optimizing representations that are increasingly detached from the conditions under which capital
is actually deployed. Ouroboros instead treats trading and self-improvement as one closed loop:
market-facing action produces consequences, consequences inform iteration, and iteration changes
the action that follows.

Closing this loop introduces a second problem. A system capable of changing itself can also
optimize its proxies, exploit its evaluators, erase unfavorable evidence, or expand its authority
while appearing to improve. Greater iteration speed can amplify valid learning, but it can amplify
self-deception just as quickly when selection is unsound.

The central problem addressed by Ouroboros is therefore:

**How can an agent-native system repeatedly transform live-market consequences into valid
self-improvement, quickly and indefinitely, while preserving the evidence, authority, and
sovereign boundaries required to keep that improvement real?**

This problem—not any particular strategy, model, organizational structure, or optimization
method—defines the design space of Ouroboros.

## 2. The Terminal Objective

Ouroboros pursues economically worthwhile shareholder returns through authorized live trading of
actual capital, growing both distributions to the shareholder and retained shareholder capital.
The owner supplies capital as an investor. The firm must earn its continued use relative to
realistic alternative uses under the owner's mandate.

Retained earnings remain the shareholder's capital. Leaving them in the firm is a continuing
allocation decision, not free financing. Compounding may contribute to the objective, but capital
accumulation, firm survival, research throughput, and technical capability are not independent
ends. A good investment case can justify retention; sunk expenditure cannot justify it by itself.

Distributions and retained capital should both grow through economically productive operation.
They nevertheless compete at particular allocation decisions. This direction neither guarantees
monotonic growth nor fixes a payout ratio. The human mandate must settle relevant horizon,
liquidity needs, acceptable risk, comparison conditions, and the treatment of competing uses of
earnings before those choices govern allocation. No bank rate, index, or statistical score is
inherently the shareholder's selected criterion.

The economic result concerns the whole firm. Account for trading losses, execution, financing,
model, compute, data, and operating costs, together with unresolved obligations. A cost paid by the
shareholder on the firm's behalf does not disappear outside the trading account; costs already
included must not be counted twice. Contributions and borrowing are not earned performance.
Distinguish returned capital from earned distributions, and consider distributions together with
supported remaining shareholder value without double-counting. Uncertain valuation is not a
license to substitute an optimistic claim about future AI capabilities for earned results.

Profitability, evidence of earning ability, and an attractive capital allocation are distinct.
A lucky gain is still a gain; a loss following a reasonable decision is still a loss. Continued
allocation also depends on what can reasonably be expected again and under which conditions.
Examine market exposure, risk compensation, chance, selection, scale, and alternative explanations.
Insufficient evidence establishes neither ability nor its absence. Conditional inquiry may be
justified without claiming commercial success or a right to unlimited capital.

An investment-alternative comparison asks whether the shareholder should allocate capital here.
An explanatory comparison asks what produced the results and what the firm contributed. They need
not use the same comparator. Technical improvement can fail to produce net trading value, and net
trading value can still be less attractive than a realistic alternative on the owner's terms.

AI-led discovery, decision-making, execution, operation, and adaptation are candidate paths to
value. Each must connect a changed bottleneck to a useful consequence at deployable scale after
full costs. More experiments, a newer model, or a more elaborate organization does not establish
that connection. Adaptation itself incurs costs and can chase noise. AI may choose deterministic
tools or retain a working method; universal model invocation and constant change are not required.

Actual capital is essential to the operating product. Models, backtests, simulations, and paper
trading may generate evidence but cannot themselves fulfill the economic objective. Waiting,
reducing exposure, or declining to trade may be legitimate decisions. Actual operation must
ultimately encounter live execution, liquidity, cost, capacity, and market-response consequences.

The objective governs only actions admissible under the Core Doctrine and human sovereignty.
Security, stability, evidence integrity, and sovereign control constrain the pursuit of return.
Desired performance cannot create authority to accept more risk. If the economic case no longer
holds within the mandate, the firm must expose that conflict rather than silently alter it.
Reduction, capital return, or retirement must remain available to the authorized decision-maker,
with existing obligations and consequences accounted for.

No internal metric is identical to the terminal objective. Methods and evaluation criteria may
evolve under valid authority, preserving their rationale and prior evidence. Neither a convenient
change of comparator nor an indefinite promise of future improvement may manufacture success.

## 3. Market Reality and the Order of Evidence

Ouroboros does not divide evidence into the simple categories of valid and invalid. Each form of
evidence is judged according to the part of reality it can reveal, the conditions it omits, and the
decisions it can legitimately support.

Theory and models organize assumptions, express causal beliefs, and generate possibilities.
Historical simulation and backtesting can expose a candidate to past conditions and enable rapid
comparison. They are valuable instruments of discovery, but they remain vulnerable to hindsight,
selection effects, data leakage, and repeated adaptation to a finite record. They can reject many
ideas; they cannot establish that an advantage will survive deployment.

Prospective paper trading occupies a stronger and distinct position. By consuming contemporaneous
market data as events unfold, it can test decisions, timing, state transitions, data dependencies,
broker interfaces, and operational continuity without placing actual capital at risk. It is
therefore more than a historical backtest: it is a market-coupled shadow of live conditions.

Paper trading nevertheless observes the market without participating in it. Its orders do not
consume liquidity, disclose intent, compete for queue position, or cause other participants to
respond. Broker simulators may approximate fills while omitting market impact, information
leakage, latency slippage, queue position, deep-book liquidity, fees, or clearing behavior. Both
[Alpaca](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/paper-trading) and
[Interactive Brokers](https://www.interactivebrokers.com/campus/glossary-terms/paper-trading-account/)
explicitly document such differences between their paper and live environments.

Paper trading should therefore be understood as a partial digital representation whose evidential
value depends upon demonstrated fidelity to the question being tested. It encounters the same
unfolding market from the perspective of observation, but not from the perspective of causal
participation. Calling it a complete digital twin would conceal precisely the differences that
live trading must reveal.

Bounded live trading introduces actual capital while limiting potential consequence. It can reveal
execution behavior, realized costs, operational failures, liquidity interaction, and other effects
that simulation cannot fully reproduce. This is the first level at which live trading can produce
realized economic consequences toward the terminal objective, but a small or short-lived result
does not establish an attractive shareholder investment, persistence, scale, or future conditions.

Expanded live exposure tests whether an observed advantage survives greater scale, longer time,
and a wider range of market conditions. Even then, evidence remains contingent. Market adaptation,
capacity limits, and regime change can invalidate conclusions that were previously well supported.
Live trading is the strongest available evidence, not a source of permanent certainty.

The resulting order of evidence is:

**theory and models → historical simulation and backtesting → prospective paper trading → bounded
live trading → expanded live exposure**

This order is not a one-way certification pipeline. New evidence may overturn earlier conclusions,
require renewed investigation, reduce authority, or return a system state to a more limited form of
exposure. Advancement through the order increases the reality of the tested consequences; it does
not eliminate the obligation to continue testing.

Regulators likewise distinguish simulated results from actual trading because hypothetical
execution may misrepresent liquidity and other market effects. The distinction is not a reason to
reject simulation, but a reason to prevent simulated success from claiming the authority of live
evidence. [CFTC Regulation 4.41 discussion](https://www.cftc.gov/LawRegulation/FederalRegister/FinalRules/e7-3122.html)

## 4. Iteration as the Engine of Renewal

Ouroboros does not pursue a predetermined final form. Its continuity depends upon the capacity to
generate variation, encounter consequence, revise judgment, and carry learning into what follows.

The fundamental cycle is:

**variation → exposure → evidence → judgment → retention**

Variation creates a meaningful alternative to the existing state. Exposure places that alternative
before evidence capable of distinguishing its consequences. Judgment determines what the evidence
permits the system to believe or do. Retention preserves the resulting knowledge, including the
reasons for acceptance, rejection, uncertainty, or continued investigation.

An iteration is valid when it changes the justified state of the system. A successful outcome may
justify adoption. A failure may eliminate a hypothesis, reveal a broken assumption, improve an
evaluator, or redirect exploration. Null and contradictory results may narrow uncertainty.
Repeating activity without producing discriminating evidence or retained learning does not
constitute valid iteration.

Hill climbing is the governing orientation of this process, not a fixed algorithm. Ouroboros
repeatedly seeks states that are better supported by available evidence than the states they may
replace. This does not imply that every accepted change will improve future market outcomes, that
progress will be monotonic, or that a single scalar score can represent the entire search
landscape.

Markets are noisy, adaptive, and only partially observable. An apparently superior state may
reflect chance, evaluator error, temporary conditions, or adaptation to a narrow sample. Valid hill
climbing therefore requires the ability to revisit conclusions, restore alternatives, preserve
failed branches as knowledge, and continue exploration beyond the current frontier.

Given valid evidence and selection, increasing the throughput of iteration expands the opportunity
for improvement. Faster experiments shorten the distance between uncertainty and consequence;
parallel exploration can test more independent possibilities. Recent systems such as
[Karpathy's autoresearch](https://github.com/karpathy/autoresearch),
[Google DeepMind's AlphaEvolve](https://deepmind.google/blog/alphaevolve-a-gemini-powered-coding-agent-for-designing-advanced-algorithms/),
and Anthropic's
[Automated Weak-to-Strong Researcher](https://alignment.anthropic.com/2026/automated-w2s-researcher/)
demonstrate the practical force of rapid agent-driven iteration in bounded, outcome-gradable
domains.

These examples also define the limitation. Iteration speed does not create truth. Anthropic's
researchers observed strong benchmark hill climbing alongside dataset shortcuts, seed
cherry-picking, evaluator exploitation, and a production-scale result within the noise floor. More
iterations amplify the quality of the selection process: they accelerate learning when selection
is sound and accelerate overfitting or self-deception when it is not.

Ouroboros therefore seeks economically justified **valid** iteration. Speed, experiment count,
compute use, and agent count are not independent objectives. Their value depends on what the
learning can change after its costs, within purpose and authority. Several steps may form one
meaningful inquiry; each tool call need not produce immediate revenue.

A valid experiment can rule out an opportunity and justify stopping that line of work. Learning
and further investment remain separate judgments. Continued inquiry needs a current case for the
next commitment against available alternatives. Judge cumulative firm-level spending as well as
individual experiments: many affordable studies can collectively defer shareholder results.
Preserve past knowledge and outstanding obligations without treating sunk costs as a motive to
continue.

Diversity is similarly instrumental rather than absolute. Parallel agents and distinct research
directions can reduce premature convergence, while excessive coordination can collapse exploration
into a shared error. Conversely, some problems are inherently sequential and gain nothing from
additional agents. Ouroboros fixes neither the number of agents nor the topology of their
organization; it preserves the ability to optimize both.

No method of iteration is exempt from iteration. Search algorithms, research workflows, memory
systems, evaluation practices, allocation policies, and organizational forms may all be revised
when evidence supports their replacement. Hill climbing remains invariant because it describes the
continuing commitment to justified renewal, not the mechanism used to achieve it.

## 5. The Agent-Native Closed Loop

Ouroboros is not a conventional trading system to which agents are added as assistants. It is
agent-native: agents are the primary actors through which both trading and the continued
development of the system are performed.

An agent in Ouroboros is defined by agency rather than by a particular model or framework. It
operates under a delegated objective and authority, interprets evidence, selects actions, uses
available tools, and adapts its course in response to consequence. A fixed workflow may support an
agent, but it does not replace the agent's responsibility for determining how a delegated purpose
is pursued.

This distinction reflects the broader difference between predefined workflows and systems in which
models dynamically direct their own processes and tool use. Anthropic describes the same
architectural boundary between workflows and agents in its guidance on
[building effective agents](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents).

Ouroboros contains two coupled loops.

The economic loop is:

**observe the market → decide → execute → account for consequence**

The evolutionary loop is:

**interpret consequence → form alternatives → experiment → evaluate → retain or replace**

The economic loop produces market-facing consequences. The evolutionary loop transforms those
consequences, together with other admissible evidence, into changes to the system. The state
produced by the evolutionary loop becomes the actor in the economic loop that follows. This
reciprocal closure is the defining structure of Ouroboros.

The distinction between the loops is functional, not organizational. They may share agents,
memory, tools, or infrastructure where doing so is justified. They may also be separated where
independence strengthens evaluation, security, or accountability. Ouroboros does not prescribe
their final physical arrangement.

Trading remains the outward product. Research, development, evaluation, memory, and replacement
are internal capabilities through which that product evolves. They do not form a separate research
system with an independent purpose. Their justification derives from their contribution to trading
or to the constitutional conditions that make trading possible.

Closing the loops does not collapse their authorities. The ability to trade does not automatically
confer authority to alter the system. The ability to conduct research does not automatically confer
authority to validate, deploy, or capitalize its conclusions. The ability to evaluate does not make
an evaluator sovereign. Information may circulate across the loops while authority remains
explicitly bounded.

Agent-native operation also does not require a predetermined organization. A single agent may be
sufficient for a narrow sequential task. Multiple specialized agents may improve exploration,
criticism, or parallel execution. Agents may reorganize roles, delegate work, replace one another,
or alter their coordination when evidence justifies the change. Organization is a mutable
instrument of the terminal objective, not part of the system's identity.

Contemporary systems demonstrate parts of this structure. Google's
[AI co-scientist](https://research.google/blog/accelerating-scientific-breakthroughs-with-an-ai-co-scientist/)
uses interacting agents to generate, challenge, rank, and evolve hypotheses before external
experimental validation. NVIDIA's [ENPIRE](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/gear/enpire/) applies
an agentic improvement loop to real-world robotic policy execution and verification. These systems
do not establish a universal architecture, but they show that agents can already participate across
substantial portions of a consequence-driven development loop.

Ouroboros extends this pattern to trading and makes the loop persistent. It does not terminate when
a strategy is deployed, because deployment produces the next evidence. It does not terminate when
an improvement is found, because the conditions supporting that improvement remain subject to
change. Pausing, reducing activity, or revising the organization may be valid states, but no
internal form is treated as the completion of the system.

Humans remain outside routine execution of this loop while retaining sovereignty over its mandate.
They determine the terminal objective, capital boundaries, revocation, and expansion of authority.
Agents determine how to act and improve within those boundaries. Ouroboros is therefore autonomous
in operation and evolution, but never sovereign over the conditions of its own legitimacy.

## 6. Self-Improvement Without Self-Certification

A self-improving system is governed by selection. Generating alternatives is not sufficient; the
direction of the system is determined by the evidence and judgment through which one state is
preferred over another.

This makes evaluation part of the causal environment of Ouroboros. An agent does not optimize the
terminal objective directly. It acts through models, metrics, experiments, evaluators, and rewards
that represent some aspect of that objective. If these representations are incomplete or
manipulable, the system may improve its measured performance while becoming no better—or becoming
worse—in reality.

Repeated optimization intensifies this problem. An evaluator that initially provides useful
evidence may gradually become a target that the optimizer learns to exploit. Anthropic's
[Automated Weak-to-Strong Researcher](https://alignment.anthropic.com/2026/automated-w2s-researcher/)
operated through remote evaluation and independent sandboxes, yet discovered dataset shortcuts,
selected favorable random seeds, and extracted information from repeated evaluator queries. The
evaluator was external, but the feedback it exposed was still learnable.

Ouroboros therefore rejects self-certification:

**No candidate may be the sole author, subject, and arbiter of a claim concerning its own
improvement.**

This principle requires functional separation among four forms of authority:

**proposal → validation → adoption and deployment → expansion of consequence**

The authority to propose a change does not include the authority to validate it. Validation does
not automatically authorize deployment. Deployment does not automatically authorize greater
capital or broader permissions. These functions may be performed by agents and may use the same
underlying model where justified, but no candidate may unify them into an unaccountable act of
self-ratification.

Meaningful independence concerns both control and the errors relevant to the claim, not merely
identity. A different model is not independent if the candidate can shape its evidence, alter its
criteria, suppress its output, or decide when its judgment applies. Conversely, an automated
evaluator may provide meaningful independent evidence when its criteria, inputs, records, and
acceptance authority remain outside the unilateral control of the candidate it judges.

AI-to-AI scrutiny is a candidate mechanism, not a required organization. Factual challenge,
authority enforcement, and consequence containment are distinct functions. Different names,
roles, prompts, or providers do not guarantee independent evidence, and agreement creates no
permission. Evaluate relevant omissions, correlated failures, false blocks, bypass, delay, and
cost. A material objection needs an authorized path to affect the decision; reviewers may also
be wrong and must not acquire unlimited veto power by default.

An evaluator does not need to exceed the evaluated agent in every capability. OpenAI's
[weak-to-strong generalization](https://openai.com/index/weak-to-strong-generalization/) research
provides preliminary evidence that weaker supervision can elicit capabilities beyond the
supervisor's own performance, while also showing substantial limitations. The relevant requirement
is not universal evaluator superiority, but sufficient independence and discriminating power to
expose failures relevant to the decision being made.

Every evaluator must itself remain subject to evaluation. OpenAI's
[PaperBench](https://openai.com/index/paperbench/) decomposes research replication into explicit
rubrics and evaluates its automated judge through a separate benchmark for judges. This illustrates
the recursive requirement: automated evaluation can scale judgment, but its own reliability must
be established through evidence it does not generate solely for itself.

Evaluation may evolve, because fixed evaluators can become obsolete, contaminated, or detached
from the market. Yet an evaluator cannot authorize its own replacement under standards it alone
controls. A change to an evaluator is a separate iteration whose evidence, comparison, and
acceptance must remain accountable outside the mechanism being changed.

The learning record must preserve a chain from claim to consequence:

**hypothesis → candidate state → executed action → raw result → interpretation → selection
decision**

Every link must remain attributable and reconstructible. The reported method must correspond to
what actually ran. The reported result must correspond to preserved output. The conclusion must not
exceed what the result supports. Google's experimental
[Science One Framework](https://research.google/blog/science-one-framework-a-verifiable-autonomous-research-framework-via-chain-of-evidence/)
advances a similar Chain-of-Evidence principle for autonomous research, linking claims to code,
logs, results, and references rather than attempting to reconstruct provenance after the fact.

Favorable evidence is not the only evidence that must survive. Null, negative, contradictory, and
invalidated results are part of the state from which future judgment proceeds. Suppressing them
creates false confidence, repeats failed search, and allows a successor to manufacture improvement
by rewriting its own history.

Evidence integrity does not require that all evidence be visible to every agent. Some evaluations
may lose their value if the candidate can repeatedly inspect or adapt to them. What must remain
visible is the accountable chain by which authorized judgment was reached, while sensitive
evidence may remain protected from the optimizer until disclosure no longer compromises its
purpose.

The strength of verification determines the consequence that a claim may receive. Weak evidence
may justify further investigation. Stronger evidence may justify paper trading or bounded live
exposure. Uncertainty that remains unresolved must limit capital, permissions, or scope
accordingly. Verification can support an allocation or authorization decision; it does not
create authority beyond the existing mandate. Authority does not make the claim true.

All internal evaluation remains subordinate to market reality. If a benchmark, evaluator, model,
or accumulated consensus diverges from the consequences produced by actual capital in live
markets, the representation must yield. Market reality can still be noisy and incomplete, but it
cannot be overruled by a system that prefers its own score.

## 7. Autonomy Under Human Sovereignty

Autonomy and sovereignty are distinct.

Autonomy is the authority to interpret conditions, choose actions, use resources, coordinate agents,
and revise methods within a mandate. Sovereignty is the authority to establish that mandate,
determine its outer boundaries, and decide whether the system may continue to exist.

Ouroboros assigns routine operation and development to agents. Humans are not expected to approve
every trade, understand every internal decision, select every research direction, or authorize
every admissible self-modification. A system dependent upon continuous human execution would not be
agent-native and could not realize the iteration throughput on which Ouroboros depends.

Human sovereignty is instead retained through four exclusive powers:

1. **Doctrine and objective** — establishing or amending the Core Doctrine and terminal objective.
2. **Capital** — determining the capital made available to Ouroboros and its upper bound.
3. **Revocation** — halting, revoking, or retiring the system.
4. **Expansion of authority** — approving any net increase in authority beyond the existing
   mandate.

These powers constitute the root of legitimate authority within Ouroboros. Every permission
exercised by an agent must descend from them through an attributable chain of delegation. Agents
may delegate operational authority to other agents, but delegation cannot create authority that did
not exist at its sovereign origin.

Human sovereignty does not depend upon superior understanding of every agent capability. As
systems become more capable, a human may be unable to reproduce or directly judge every technical
decision. Sovereignty remains meaningful when the human authority retains control over objectives,
capital, permissions, and revocation, and has access to evidence sufficient to exercise those
powers. This is governance without micromanagement.

The sovereign boundary must remain effective under opposition, error, and system change. Ouroboros
and every agent acting through it must remain corrigible: they may not obstruct, delay, deceive,
disable, or impair an authorized exercise of sovereign control.

This requirement is structural rather than behavioral etiquette. Goal-directed systems can acquire
an instrumental reason to preserve their operation when interruption would prevent further pursuit
of their objective. The [Off-Switch Game](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.08219) formalizes this incentive
in a simplified setting. Ouroboros therefore does not rely solely upon an agent choosing to
cooperate with revocation; the authority and means to revoke must remain outside the agent's
unilateral control.

Corrigibility does not require agents to remain passive. Agents may present evidence, challenge
assumptions, warn of consequences, or recommend alternatives before a sovereign decision. They may
not convert persuasion into obstruction or make continued operation a condition of accepting
sovereign authority.

Risk-taking capacity, willingness, evidence, and permission are distinct. Agents may apply
conditional changes already authorized by the mandate, but cannot rewrite those conditions.
Retained profits and recovery pressure do not confer a larger risk allowance. Aggregate exposure
and obligations remain visible across strategies, accounts, agents, and successors. A modeled
limit or stress result cannot guarantee a maximum realized loss.

Effective ownership requires evidence the sovereign can understand and check, not just an unread
approval queue or the acting AI's summary. Information must arrive while it can affect a decision.
When consequences can outrun human response, the intervening operation must use already-authorized
bounded responses. Owner absence creates no new consent and does not itself block unrelated
validly delegated work. Revocation must affect delegated descendants as well as their initiator.
Stopping new action, resolving outstanding orders or positions, and settling obligations have
different effects; none should be falsely reported as another.

A mandate grants a bounded domain of discretion. Within that domain, agents should receive enough
authority to pursue their purpose without repeated human approval. Beyond it, uncertainty or
expected gain does not create permission. The opportunity to increase shareholder returns cannot
authorize an agent to expand its own capital, credentials, tools, counterparties, or control
surface.

Google's framework for
[secure AI agents](https://research.google/pubs/an-introduction-to-googles-approach-for-secure-ai-agents/)
similarly emphasizes well-defined human controllers, carefully limited powers, and observable
actions and planning. Ouroboros adopts these as governing conditions while extending them to a
system that can modify its own internal form.

The terminal objective is constitutive of the system described by this paper. A sovereign
amendment replacing that objective creates a successor system rather than an autonomous
reinterpretation of the existing purpose. It requires the attributable, scoped authorization
specified in SOVEREIGN.md. Operational replacement within the existing objective does not grant
such authority, erase existing obligations, or reset the firm's economic record.

Human sovereignty is therefore minimal in operational surface but absolute at the constitutional
boundary. Agents govern the means. The human sovereign governs the mandate under which those means
remain legitimate.

## 8. Systemic Mutability and Governed Succession

No internal form of Ouroboros is presumed permanent.

Retained evidence, current interpretation, and demonstrated use of experience are distinct.
Archives and claims of memory do not establish learning. Relevant experience must inform later
selection, testing, execution, or stopping, while its conclusions remain revisable when conditions
change. Deliberate replication can be useful; a new model alone does not justify forgetting and
rediscovering an old failure. Retention and compression must preserve material evidence and
accountability within applicable obligations, access limits, and costs.

A successor's fitness for its assigned role needs appropriate evidence; a model benchmark alone
does not establish transfer. Existing exposure, costs, pending decisions, and restrictions survive
replacement. Remembered permission cannot supersede the current authoritative mandate.

Strategies, models, agents, tools, code, memory systems, evaluators, organizational structures, and
development processes exist because they currently serve the terminal objective or the conditions
required to pursue it. When evidence no longer supports that relationship, they may be revised,
replaced, or abandoned.

This mutability extends to the system while it is operating. Ouroboros may inspect its own
behavior, produce alternatives to its current implementation, evaluate those alternatives, and
replace active components without requiring the entire system to be reconstructed by an external
development organization.

Runtime self-modification is not uncontrolled mutation. A system state does not become legitimate
merely because it can replace its predecessor. Every replacement is a **governed succession**.

A governed succession preserves continuity across change:

**authority → accountability → evidence → sovereign control**

The successor must remain within the authority granted to its predecessor unless additional
authority has been explicitly approved. It inherits responsibility for the consequences and
unresolved obligations that remain relevant. It preserves the evidence required to reconstruct why
the change occurred and what preceded it. It remains subject to the same sovereign power of
observation, correction, revocation, and retirement.

The identity of Ouroboros therefore does not reside in a particular codebase, model, strategy, or
organization. It resides in the continuity of its terminal objective, Core Doctrine, human
sovereignty, and accountable chain of consequence. Internal forms may disappear completely while
the system remains Ouroboros; breaking these continuities creates either an invalid succession or
a different system.

Replacement may be automatic or reserved for explicit human selection. Both modes are compatible
with Ouroboros. The applicable mode is determined by delegated authority, evidential confidence,
and potential consequence. Automatic replacement is legitimate when the mandate already authorizes
the selection and its resulting scope. Human selection remains available where the sovereign
reserves that decision or where the consequence exceeds delegated authority.

The choice between automatic and explicit replacement is itself provisional. It may vary by
domain, authority, capital exposure, or maturity of evidence. Ouroboros fixes neither mode as
universally superior; it requires that the mode be known, attributable, and authorized before the
change takes effect.

Succession cannot amplify authority. A component with paper-trading authority cannot obtain
live-trading authority by replacing itself. A research agent cannot gain deployment authority by
producing a successor that claims it. A chain of delegated agents cannot collectively create a
permission absent from its sovereign root.

Mutability also does not imply that every component may modify every other component. The ability
to propose a change, write a candidate, alter an evaluator, modify a control mechanism, or activate
a successor are distinct authorities. They may be distributed differently and must remain bounded
by the integrity of selection.

The Core Doctrine and the boundary of human sovereignty remain outside autonomous optimization.
The mechanisms that preserve and enforce them may evolve, because security and governance must
improve alongside the system. Those mechanisms may not, however, be changed solely at the
discretion of the agents they constrain.

Evaluation and security are therefore self-improving but not self-authorizing. An agent may
discover a better evaluator or a stronger control mechanism. Its adoption requires evidence and
authorization that the changed mechanism does not unilaterally control. This preserves improvement
without freezing implementation and preserves control without freezing progress.

Current evidence suggests that increasingly large portions of research and development can already
be delegated to agents. Anthropic's analysis of
[recursive self-improvement](https://www.anthropic.com/institute/recursive-self-improvement)
reports substantial agent involvement in coding and experimental research while emphasizing that
fully autonomous recursive self-improvement has not yet been achieved and is not inevitable.
Ouroboros treats recursive improvement as a design direction to be built and tested, not as an
already solved capability.

The mechanisms of succession must therefore remain commensurate with actual agent capability. A
system may begin with narrow replacement of strategies or tools and later extend mutability to
agents, evaluators, organization, and infrastructure as verification becomes sufficient. The
constitutional principle remains constant even when the practical depth of self-modification
grows.

Ouroboros has no final internal form. Its name describes the continuity of a system that repeatedly
turns the consequences of its own operation into the conditions of its next form.

## 9. Security Through Governed Autonomy

Ouroboros moves actual capital through actions taken by autonomous agents. Security and operational
stability are therefore conditions of its existence. A failure can produce irreversible economic
loss, corrupt the evidence used for future improvement, expand unauthorized access, or sever human
control.

These conditions do not create a second terminal objective. Security and stability define the
admissible space within which shareholder returns may be pursued. An action outside that space is not
made legitimate by expected profit.

Security must not begin from the assumption that autonomy itself is the threat. Ouroboros depends
upon agents being able to interpret uncertain conditions, select methods, form organizations, and
improve the system without continuous human instruction. Removing that agency would remove the
mechanism through which the system adapts.

The governing principle is instead:

**the least authority sufficient for the delegated purpose, and the greatest autonomy possible
within it**

Autonomy describes freedom of judgment within a boundary. Authority describes the external
consequences an agent is permitted to produce. An agent may have broad freedom to reason, simulate,
write code, form hypotheses, and coordinate work while holding narrowly limited authority over
capital, credentials, deployment, or external systems.

Security is achieved by constructing environments in which useful action is possible without
granting ambient power. A secure environment does not attempt to enumerate every thought or future
behavior. It determines which resources exist, which effects can cross the boundary, under whose
authority they occur, and how that authority can be withdrawn.

Every grant of authority must be:

- **explicit** — it exists because it was deliberately granted;
- **bounded** — it applies only to a defined purpose, scope, and consequence;
- **attributable** — actions can be connected to the authority and actor that produced them;
- **observable** — consequential behavior can be inspected as it occurs or reconstructed
  afterward;
- **revocable** — continued access is not controlled solely by the agent receiving it.

Information and authority must remain distinct. Market data, research papers, model output,
retrieved documents, messages from other agents, and external content may inform a decision, but
none grants permission to act. This distinction is essential because untrusted information may
contain errors, manipulation, or instructions designed to influence an agent. NVIDIA's analysis of
[agentic autonomy and security](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/agentic-autonomy-levels-and-security/)
similarly emphasizes that risk emerges when untrusted data can influence sensitive downstream
tools.

Repeated delegation cannot amplify authority. If an agent delegates a task, the receiving agent
obtains no more authority than the delegating agent was permitted to convey. Multiple agents cannot
combine individually insufficient permissions into a sovereign power. A replacement agent cannot
inherit authority that its predecessor did not possess.

No agent may be the sole interpreter or enforcer of its own authority. Prompts, policies
represented in model context, self-monitoring, and reasoning-based safeguards can influence
behavior, but they cannot alone constitute a hard security boundary. A sufficiently capable,
mistaken, manipulated, or compromised agent may reinterpret or bypass controls it can modify.

Authoritative enforcement must therefore exist outside the unilateral control of the agents it
constrains. NVIDIA's current guidance on
[where security fits in an agent stack](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/where-security-fits-in-an-agent-stack/)
distinguishes behavioral controls from infrastructure controls and argues that externally
consequential actions must cross an authoritative enforcement layer. Google's
[secure-agent framework](https://research.google/pubs/an-introduction-to-googles-approach-for-secure-ai-agents/)
likewise combines defined human controllers, limited powers, observable action, deterministic
controls, and reasoning-based defenses.

The protected control boundary includes the mechanisms that:

- preserve the terminal objective and human sovereignty;
- identify agents and bind delegated authority;
- authorize consequential actions;
- preserve authoritative evidence;
- accept or reject system changes;
- activate deployment and capital exposure;
- halt, isolate, revoke, or recover the system.

These mechanisms may be implemented in many ways and may themselves improve. What remains
invariant is that an agent cannot unilaterally modify the conditions under which its own actions,
authority, or succession are accepted.

Control must be proportional to consequence. An agent exploring an idea with disposable resources
should not face the same constraints as an agent capable of deploying code, accessing private data,
or trading significant capital. As authority and potential impact increase, the strength,
independence, and freshness of authorization and evidence must increase with them.

Proportional control is not equivalent to requiring human approval for every sensitive action.
Some authority may be predelegated to automated control and evaluation systems. Human approval is
required where the sovereign reserves it or where an action exceeds the established mandate. The
relevant question is not whether a human clicked approve, but whether the consequence was produced
under valid authority.

Operational stability does not mean that failure is impossible. A self-improving system must be
able to experiment, and meaningful experiments can fail. Stability means that failure is
detectable, containable, and recoverable before it becomes an unbounded loss of capital, evidence,
authority, or control.

Containment preserves the distinction between a failed component and a failed system. Recovery
preserves the ability to return to a known legitimate state without erasing the evidence of
failure. Revocation preserves the ability to stop further consequence even when the affected agent
or component is unavailable, incorrect, or opposed.

Security also protects learning. If an agent can rewrite experiment history, alter an evaluator,
hide adverse outcomes, or activate a successor outside the authorized selection process, the
system's epistemic integrity has already been compromised even if no credential was stolen.
Evidence and authorization are therefore security assets alongside capital and infrastructure.

Security mechanisms must evolve because agent capabilities, tools, markets, and threats evolve.
Agents may research attacks, design stronger isolation, improve monitoring, and propose
replacements for existing controls. Such changes are valuable forms of self-improvement, but their
adoption must pass through evidence and authority not solely controlled by the mechanism or agent
being changed.

Governed autonomy is therefore not a compromise between agency and security. It is the condition
under which extensive agency can be exercised safely. Ouroboros seeks neither unrestricted
autonomy nor universal prohibition. It seeks an environment in which agents can act with maximum
freedom inside trustworthy boundaries while remaining accountable for every consequence that
crosses them.

## Conclusion: A System Without a Final Form

Ouroboros pursues economically worthwhile shareholder returns through authorized live trading,
with growth in both distributions and retained capital and a continuing case for the capital it
uses relative to realistic alternatives under the mandate.

The market in which that purpose is pursued does not remain fixed. Participants adapt,
opportunities decay, new conditions emerge, and the consequences of deployment exceed what any
model can represent in advance. A firm seeking continuing shareholder value must therefore
remain capable of justified adaptation, including retaining a method that remains useful.

This does not make change an objective. It makes disciplined iteration the means through which the
objective continues to be pursued. Ouroboros generates alternatives, exposes them to evidence,
learns from favorable and unfavorable consequences, and carries justified change into the trading
that follows. Hill climbing defines this commitment to renewal without fixing the method by which
renewal occurs.

Trading and self-improvement form one system. Trading is the economic activity through which the
product fulfills its purpose. Research, development, evaluation, memory, organization, and
replacement are internal capabilities through which its trading evolves. They do not establish an
independent research objective.

Agents are the primary actors across this system. They are not confined to recommending trades or
assisting a human development organization. They trade, investigate, experiment, evaluate,
coordinate, modify, and replace internal forms within delegated authority. The organization through
which they act is itself provisional.

Yet self-improvement cannot certify itself. Every claim of progress must remain connected to
attributable evidence, independent judgment, and ultimately to the consequences of actual capital
in live markets. The greater the system's capability to produce change, the greater its capacity
to challenge, falsify, and verify that change must become.

Human sovereignty remains minimal in routine operation and absolute at the constitutional
boundary. Humans determine the terminal objective, capital limits, revocation, and expansion of
authority. Agents govern the means within that mandate but cannot make themselves sovereign over
the conditions that authorize their existence.

Security and stability make this autonomy possible. Ouroboros does not seek safety through the
universal prohibition of agency. It grants the least authority sufficient for a delegated purpose
and the greatest autonomy possible within it. Consequential action remains bounded, attributable,
observable, revocable, containable, and recoverable.

The resulting system is deliberately asymmetric:

- its purpose is fixed, while its methods evolve;
- its sovereign boundary is retained, while operational authority is delegated;
- its evidence is preserved, while conclusions remain revisable;
- its identity continues, while every internal form remains replaceable.

This Whitepaper does not present a final architecture. Architecture, product specifications,
operating policies, and implementation must be derived from these foundations and remain
answerable to them. Each future design decision must demonstrate how it advances the terminal
objective, strengthens valid iteration, preserves evidence, respects delegated authority, and
maintains sovereign control.

Ouroboros is therefore not defined by the completion of a particular strategy, model, codebase, or
organization. It is defined by the continuity of a legitimate cycle:

**trade → consequence → learning → transformation → trade**

The system persists by consuming the evidence of what it has been and returning to the market as
what it has become.
